Goals as a setup step, or goals as the spine.

A senior-design comparison of two information architectures for Kustomer's agentic CX platform — Prototype A, where goals are the input to a 5-step wizard and ongoing work returns to a per-automation surface, and Prototype B, where goals are the permanent organizing unit and monitoring/improvement live in a rich Performance area. Includes a hybrid recommendation, not a winner-takes-all answer.

Prototype AGoals Based AI Outcome (setup-wizard model)
Prototype BHybrid Goals-First Prototype (goal-as-spine)
AuthorSenior UX critique, prepared for Amisha
ScopeOnboarding · ongoing management · enterprise scale

01Executive summary

The two prototypes resolve the same brief — make Kustomer AI easier to configure and more goal-driven — by inverting each other's bet. A optimizes for the first 30 minutes; B optimizes for the next 12 months. Neither is wrong; the right answer is to keep A's intake clarity and graduate users into B's outcome-spine for ongoing operations.

Prototype A

Goals as a 5-step setup wizard.

Set Goals · Build · Test & Evaluate · Deploy · Analyze sit in a stepper. After setup, work returns to a tool-first surface (Refund Order / Manage Automations / Settings).

Where it wins

  • Strongest first-run path of either design — single form, single CTA, very low cognitive load.
  • Auto-derivation cascade is real: goals × topics × audience produce procedures, monitors, scenarios.
  • Step indicator (Step 2 of 5, blue chip) gives clear progress + reassurance.
  • Audience pills (AIC blue / AIR purple) consistently labelled on every artifact.
  • Setup Assistant in-place during intake reduces the "ask a human" friction.

Where it breaks down

  • Goals disappear from the nav once the wizard ends — users lose the frame they used to configure.
  • Sub-step nav inside Set Goals (Build/Test/Deploy/Analyze) collides with the legacy top-level Build/Test/Deploy concepts.
  • Manage Automations + per-automation cards remind users this is still tool-first under the surface.
  • No home for Quality Monitors, Suggestions, Anomaly Detection, or Computed Fields from the M1 spec.
  • Performance is one screen — fine for week one, thin at month six.
Prototype B (Hybrid)

Goals as the permanent spine, slide-out for config, Performance for depth.

Get Started wizard is the front door; Goals Home + a lightweight goal slide-out replace the 11-tab workspace; Performance hosts Quality Monitors, Suggestions, Anomalies, Reports — filterable by goal.

Where it wins

  • Outcomes are the organizing unit forever — matches the strategic brief past day one.
  • Goal slide-out is honest with M1: thin config, jump-links to depth — no over-promised editor.
  • Tabs in Performance map 1:1 to canonical M1 primitives (Quality Monitors, Suggestions, Anomalies, Reports).
  • Shared-resources model (Procedures, Knowledge, Tools, Guardrails) scales — goals reference, don't own.
  • Legacy preserved under Advanced with a LEGACY pill — honest migration path.
  • State-driven color (red wash for "needs attention") gives instant orientation.

Where it strains

  • Drilling into a goal's monitors now requires a Performance jump with a goal filter — one extra hop.
  • "Plan editing lives in Resources" is structurally clean but takes a moment to land for first-time admins.
  • No persistent KPI hero per-goal — the slide-out is purposely sparse.
  • Setup Assistant is no longer present during the wizard — only the Goals Assistant in the rail.

The short version

Prototype A is the better first hour. Prototype B is the better first year. A's wizard is a near-perfect intake — keep it. B's outcome spine is the only structure that survives a customer asking "how is my AI doing on CSAT?" six months in — keep it. Where they conflict — what happens after setup ends — graduate the user into B.

04Strengths, friction, and missed opportunities

An opinionated read of what works, what hurts, and what each prototype didn't build but could have.

Prototype A

What works · what creates friction

What works well

  • The single-form intake — explicit "Skip & configure manually" affordance respects power users.
  • Numbered sections (1–8) make completion progress visible without a stepper.
  • The "Step 2 of 5" pill in the Build view ties wizard steps to real product surfaces.
  • Setup Assistant inline answers ("Suggest goals for my business", "What should escalate to a human?") demonstrate AI competency at the moment of need.
  • Test Categories with pass/warning/fail and last-run timestamps mirror a real CI pattern admins recognize.

What creates friction

  • Wizard sub-steps Build/Test/Deploy/Analyze are ambiguous against legacy global Build/Test/Deploy concepts — they read like two different products.
  • "Refund Order" appearing as a fixed nav card hard-codes one automation example into the IA.
  • Performance is a single scrollable dashboard that mixes per-goal KPIs, insights, and topic performance — overwhelming and undelineated.
  • Quality Monitors / Anomalies are nowhere — admins who read the M1 spec will look and not find them.
  • After the wizard, there's no obvious "next" — what does an admin do on day 8?

Missed opportunities

  • Plan-diff suggestions ("we think you should tighten this guardrail") — invisible.
  • Per-goal trend reporting tied to GoalProgressSnapshot — buried in a single combined view.
  • No "Improve" surface — observations don't translate into actions inside the IA.
Prototype B

What works · what creates friction

What works well

  • Get Started is the default landing — empty-state = guided intake — and the wizard runs full-width.
  • Goals Home rows are instantly scannable: name · state pill · target · current · progress bar · suggestions count.
  • Goal slide-out resolves the "what is this goal made of?" question without committing to a workspace.
  • Plan section in the slide-out names the 4 ingredients with one-line definitions + examples in quotes.
  • "Suggest a change" CTA at the bottom of the Plan section turns the slide-out into an actionable surface.
  • Performance tabs are 1:1 with M1 canonical primitives — no invented surfaces.
  • Resources is the shared library with "attached to N goals" — blast-radius is visible at edit time.

What creates friction

  • "Plan editing lives in Resources" requires a mental model — first-time admins may try to edit in the slide-out.
  • The wizard's exit point lands on a slide-out, not a celebration screen — moment-of-success is muted.
  • No persistent per-goal KPI hero — to see live numbers you must click into Performance.
  • Goals nav is not expandable — to switch goals you go back to Goals Home (one extra click vs A's per-automation drill-in).

Missed opportunities

  • Setup Assistant inline copy during the wizard is gone — Goals Assistant in the rail is less integrated.
  • "You're live" celebration screen could surface first-monitor reading instead of a TBD placeholder.
  • Cross-goal comparison view — "show me CSAT across all goals" — not yet a destination.

07Recommended hybrid

Not "pick one." The hybrid is Prototype B's structure + Prototype A's intake quality + restored Setup Assistant + one new destination (Improve). Below: what to keep from A, what to take from B as-is, and what to merge.

Keep from A

  • The 5-step wizard — Set Goals · Build · Test & Evaluate · Deploy · Analyze, with the visible step indicator pill.
  • Setup Assistant inline during the wizard — "Suggest goals", "What should escalate?", "Draft a tone" prompts at the moment of need.
  • Audience pills (AIC blue / AIR purple) carried through every goal, procedure, monitor, test.
  • Numbered sections (1–8) in the wizard form — progress is visible without a stepper.
  • Test Categories with pass / warning / fail visual treatment — the green Continue-to-Deploy gating is excellent.

Take from B as-is

  • Goals as the spine — Goals Home is the landing after first run; the slide-out replaces a heavy workspace.
  • Performance subnav with canonical M1 tabs (Quality Monitors · Suggestions · Anomalies · Reports).
  • Resources as shared library with "attached to N goals" chips and the "Plan editing lives here" banner.
  • Settings folds AIC + AIR surfaces — they are not top-level peers.
  • Advanced [LEGACY] for the tool-first surface — preserved, not promoted.
  • State-driven color — red wash for goals needing attention, restraint elsewhere.

Merge / add new

  • Setup Assistant returns to the wizard as inline panel (lift from A) — replaces the rail-only Goals Assistant during intake.
  • Promote "Improve" to a destination — currently buried in Performance/Suggestions. A dedicated cross-goal Improve view ranks plan diffs by impact.
  • Per-goal "open in fullscreen" escape hatch on the slide-out for power users who want the depth back temporarily.
  • "You're live" analyze step shows first monitor reading or expected baseline, not "TBD".
  • Wizard exits to a fullscreen "Welcome to your goal" page (single screen with goal hero, plan summary, first monitor) → THEN to the slide-out on close.
  • Goals nav supports a small "pinned goal" sub-list (max 3 user-favorited) for daily power users who do want one-click goal switching.

09Strategic verdict

One direction, three actions.

Go with Prototype B as the structural foundation.

It scales linearly with goal count, names the M1 primitives correctly, gives enterprise buyers a credible governance story, and matches the role-based mental model of every persona except the very-first-hour admin.

Action 1 · Restore the Setup Assistant inline during the wizard. This is the single biggest thing A does better. The Goals Assistant in the rail is too easy to ignore during a focused intake task. Bring back the inline panel with the same three suggestion prompts.

Action 2 · Add a "Welcome to your goal" celebration screen between Analyze (step 5) and the goal slide-out. Show the first live monitor reading or expected baseline, the plan summary, and a single "Open my goal" CTA. The wizard exit should feel like a launch, not a pop-in.

Action 3 · Promote Improve to a top-level destination, or at minimum to a permanent slot in the Performance subnav with its own badge count. Suggestions are the agent's most demonstrable intelligence — they should not be buried.

Everything else in B is correct as drawn. Ship the spine; iterate the surface.
